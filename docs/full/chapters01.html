Chapter One

Navigating M&A Best Practices in Utility-Scale Renewable Energy Development

The renewable energy sector is evolving rapidly, with M&A activities playing a pivotal role in shaping the industry’s future. Whether you are an industry veteran or new to the field, this book aims to provide valuable insights into the complexities and nuances of acquiring and developing renewable energy projects.

As companies strive to meet ambitious pipeline goals and capitalize on emerging opportunities, understanding M&A best practices becomes essential. This book will guide you through critical aspects of the M&A process, from verifying land control to fostering collaboration between teams. We will address the following:

1. Introduction to M&A Trends in Renewable Energy

We will explore the current surge in M&A activities within the renewable energy sector. And we will explain why companies opt for acquisitions over greenfield development and how this trend impacts project timelines and market entry strategies.

2. The Critical First Step—Verifying Land Control

We will explain why verifying land control is essential and detail steps to confirm ownership and avoid legal issues.

3. Ensuring Correct Ownership and Signatories

We will dive into the complexities of ownership verification, including life estates and joint ownership. We will describe how to Develop and implement strategies to verify the identification of all legitimate owners and secure their signatures on required agreements.

4. Navigating Local Risks—Mineral Rights and Beyond

We will examine regional risks that can affect project viability, such as mineral rights issues and state-specific regulations. We will provide insights on how to identify and mitigate these risks effectively.

5. Effective Due Diligence Strategies Under Time Constraints

You will learn strategies for conducting thorough due diligence within tight M&A timelines. We will provide guidance on utilizing existing documentation and identifying key areas that can improve efficiency.

6. Environmental and Site Considerations

You will learn about assessing environmental factors that can impact a project, such as floodplains and protected habitats. We will explain how to incorporate these considerations into the M&A process using public records and specialist input.

7. Unveiling Hidden Ownership Complexities

We will uncover the challenges posed by complex ownership structures like life estates and trusts. And we will guide you on navigating these complexities to ensure lease validity and project insurability.

8. Bridging the Gap—Collaboration Between Commercial and Development Teams

Here we will address the potential friction between M&A teams and development teams. You will learn strategies to enhance collaboration, align objectives, and improve project outcomes.

9. Valuation and Timing—Assessing Project Worth Accurately

In this section we will explore how due diligence findings and project timing affect valuation. We will examine how to adjust valuations based on risks to ensure accurate assessments aligned with COD targets.

10. The Perils and Opportunities of Inheriting Existing Projects

We will examine the challenges and benefits of inheriting projects from other developers. Additionally, you will acquire strategies for managing inherited tasks, utilizing established relationships, and allocating resources efficiently.

Successful M&A comes from checking many boxes. Sometimes, it is necessary to reject commercially promising projects if they exceed the development team’s capabilities. Due diligence, collaboration, and proactive risk management are essential for successful M&A outcomes.

Riding the Wave - Current M&A Trends in Renewable Energy

The renewable energy sector is experiencing a dynamic shift, with mergers and acquisitions becoming a cornerstone strategy for growth and expansion. This trend reflects the industry’s maturation and the increasing complexity of developing large-scale renewable projects.

Why Companies Prefer M&A Over Greenfield Development

Greenfield development means beginning from the ground up, including site selection, permitting, financing, and construction, which can take years. In comparison, acquiring existing projects offers various advantages:

  • Time Efficiency: Expedites operational readiness by eliminating initial development phases.
  • Risk Mitigation: Reduces exposure to development risks like permitting delays or community opposition.
  • Strategic Positioning: Allows companies to quickly enter new markets or technologies.
  • Resource Optimization: Enables better allocation of resources by focusing on operational efficiency rather than development hurdles.

Solar projects typically require five to seven years, while wind projects can take seven to ten years, not including permitting on public lands. Public lands can involve various stakeholders such as the Bureau of Land Management, or the Bureau of Indian Affairs. The process of securing land rights may be significantly prolonged when numerous stakeholders are engaged.

M&A lets you skip the initial development phase, saving a great deal of time and risk. An acquired project lets you avoid early-stage uncertainties and achieve revenue sooner.

M&A Projects Span a Wide Spectrum of Development Stages

  • Pre-construction projects possess land leases and may have obtained necessary permits; however, construction activities have not yet commenced.
  • Under-Construction Projects: Physical construction has started, reducing uncertainty around project realization.
  • Operational Projects: Already generating energy and revenue, providing immediate cash flow.

Market Drivers Behind the M&A Surge

  • Regulatory Incentives: Policies like the Investment Tax Credit (ITC) and Production Tax Credit (PTC) in the U.S. incentivize timely project completion.
  • Market Demand: Corporations and utilities are increasing renewable energy procurement to meet sustainability goals.
  • Financial Markets: Low interest rates and high liquidity make financing acquisitions more attractive.
  • Technological Maturity: Advances in technology reduce operational risks, making existing projects more valuable.

Industry Observations

At a recent conference, we participated in several discussions that focused on M&A strategies. Many companies are now interested in acquiring projects past the initial stage.

This reflects a strategic shift where companies aim to enhance their portfolios quickly to meet market demands and investor expectations.

Understanding these trends is essential for stakeholders in the renewable energy sector. Companies considering M&A must navigate complex due diligence processes to ensure successful acquisitions.
