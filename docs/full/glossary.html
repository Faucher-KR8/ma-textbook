Glossary of Key Terms

An Insider’s Guide to Mastering Mergers, Acquisitions & Power Plant Development

ACOE – Army Corps of Engineers. Federal agency with jurisdiction over Waters of the US, including wetlands.

ALTA Survey – American Land Title Association survey. A comprehensive boundary survey required by most title insurers for commercial transactions.

Avoidance – Design strategy to site project infrastructure away from environmentally sensitive areas (wetlands, habitat) to eliminate permit requirements.

Bankability – The ability of a project to secure project finance debt. A bankable project has clear title, valid permits, an executed PPA, and a manageable risk profile.

Beginning of Construction (BOC) – IRS concept that determines eligibility for ITC and PTC incentives. Safe harbor rules allow projects to lock in incentive rates based on the BOC date.

BESS – Battery Energy Storage System. A grid-connected system that stores electricity for later dispatch to the grid.

CEQA – California Environmental Quality Act. California’s state-level environmental review law, analogous to NEPA at the federal level.

CFADS – Cash Flow Available for Debt Service. A key metric in project finance; the numerator in the DSCR calculation.

COD – Commercial Operation Date. The date a project begins commercial energy production. Many incentives, milestones, and contractual provisions are tied to COD.

Data Room – A secure, access-controlled repository—usually virtual—where a seller organizes title, permit, financial, and contract documents for buyer review during due diligence.

DCF – Discounted Cash Flow. A valuation method that projects a project’s future cash flows and discounts them to present value.

Domestic Content Bonus – An increase to the ITC or PTC available to projects that source a required percentage of their steel, iron, and manufactured components from U.S. producers.

DSCR – Debt Service Coverage Ratio. The ratio of CFADS to debt service payments in a given period. A DSCR below 1.0 means the project cannot service its debt from operating cash flow.

Earnout – A portion of the purchase price paid after closing, contingent on the acquired project or company meeting defined performance milestones (such as reaching COD or a production target).

Energy Community – A location-based bonus category that increases the ITC or PTC for projects sited in qualifying areas, such as former coal, oil, or gas communities and brownfield sites.

EPC – Engineering, Procurement, and Construction. An EPC contract is a fixed-price, fixed-schedule contract with a single contractor responsible for design, procurement, and construction.

Escrow / Holdback – Funds withheld from the purchase price at closing—held by a third party (escrow) or retained by the buyer (holdback)—to cover potential post-closing claims, such as breaches of reps and warranties.

FEOC – Foreign Entity of Concern. A restricted category of foreign entities—broadly, those owned or controlled by, or otherwise connected to, China, Russia, Iran, or North Korea—whose involvement can disqualify a project from federal clean energy tax credits. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) of 2025 expanded FEOC restrictions, previously limited to the electric vehicle credit, to the ITC, PTC, and advanced manufacturing credit, and added “material assistance” tests tied to where a project’s components are sourced. FEOC compliance has become a core diligence item in renewable energy M&A. (Rules and effective dates are phasing in and subject to Treasury guidance.)

ICA – Interconnection Agreement. The contract between a project and the transmission system operator governing the project’s connection to the grid.

IRA – Inflation Reduction Act. The 2022 federal law that substantially expanded and extended clean energy tax credits, including the ITC and PTC, and introduced credit transferability and bonus adders.

IRR – Internal Rate of Return. The discount rate at which a project’s net present value (NPV) equals zero; a common measure of expected project return.

ITC – Investment Tax Credit. A federal tax credit equal to a percentage of qualifying project costs, taken in the year the project is placed in service.

LCOE – Levelized Cost of Energy. The average per-MWh cost to build and operate a project over its lifetime.

LOI – Letter of Intent. A preliminary, generally non-binding document outlining the principal terms of a proposed transaction before definitive agreements are negotiated.

MAC / MAE – Material Adverse Change / Material Adverse Effect. A contractual standard defining events serious enough to let a buyer renegotiate or walk away from a transaction before closing.

MACRS – Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System. IRS depreciation schedule that allows most renewable assets to be depreciated over 5 years (vs. 20+ years straight-line).

Material Assistance – Under the FEOC rules, a test based on the share of a project’s component costs attributable to prohibited foreign entities; exceeding the applicable threshold can disqualify the project from tax credits.

Mineral Rights – The legal right to extract minerals, oil, gas, and metals from below the surface of land.

MOR – Mineral Ownership Report. A specialized title search of the subsurface (mineral) estate—parallel to a surface title commitment—that traces mineral ownership, often back to the original severance deed, to identify who controls the minerals beneath a project.

NEPA – National Environmental Policy Act. Federal law requiring environmental review of federal agency actions, including permits for projects on federal land.

NPV – Net Present Value. The present value of a project’s future cash flows minus the initial investment; a positive NPV indicates the project is expected to create value for investors.

NTP – Notice to Proceed. Authorization from the project owner to the EPC contractor to begin construction.

O&M – Operations and Maintenance.

OBBBA – One Big Beautiful Bill Act. The 2025 federal law that revised several clean energy tax credits and significantly expanded the FEOC restrictions applicable to them.

Offtaker – The purchaser of a project’s energy or capacity under a PPA, such as a utility, corporate buyer, or other creditworthy counterparty.

P50 / P90 – Probability exceedance estimates for energy production. P50 = 50% probability of exceedance (median); P90 = 90% probability of exceedance (conservative).

Partnership Flip – A tax equity structure in which the tax equity investor receives a majority of tax benefits until reaching a target return, then the project sponsor’s economic interest increases (“flips”).

Phase I ESA – Phase I Environmental Site Assessment. A standardized investigation of a property’s environmental condition and history used to identify Recognized Environmental Conditions (RECs) before acquisition or financing.

PPA – Power Purchase Agreement. A long-term contract for the sale of energy from a project to a utility, corporate buyer, or other offtaker.

PSA – Purchase and Sale Agreement. The definitive contract governing the sale of a project or company, setting out purchase price, representations and warranties, covenants, and closing conditions.

PTC – Production Tax Credit. A per-kWh federal tax credit on energy produced during the first 10 years of a project’s operation.

PWA – Prevailing Wage and Apprenticeship. Labor requirements a project must satisfy to qualify for the full (five-times) ITC or PTC rate rather than the reduced base rate.

REC – Recognized Environmental Condition (in Phase I ESA context). Also: Renewable Energy Certificate.

Recapture – The clawback of previously claimed ITC benefits if the project is sold or ceases qualifying use within the five-year recapture period following placement in service.

Repowering – Replacement of major project equipment (turbines, inverters) to extend asset life, increase output, or qualify for new tax incentives.

ROW – Right-of-Way. The legal right to cross or use another party’s land—public or private—for project access, transmission, or related infrastructure.

RWI – Representations and Warranties Insurance. Insurance coverage for losses arising from seller breaches of reps and warranties in the PSA.

SCADA – Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition. Real-time monitoring system for project performance.

SPV – Special Purpose Vehicle. A standalone legal entity created to own a single project, isolating its financing and risk from the developer’s broader balance sheet.

Surface Use Agreement – A contract between a surface rights holder and a mineral rights holder governing how mineral operations are conducted on the surface.

Tax Equity – Investor capital contributed to a project primarily in exchange for its federal tax credits and depreciation benefits, commonly deployed through structures such as the partnership flip.

Title Commitment – A preliminary agreement by a title insurer to issue title insurance, subject to specified exceptions and conditions.

Transferability – The ability, established under the IRA (Section 6418), to sell certain clean energy tax credits to unrelated third parties for cash, providing an alternative to traditional tax equity.

Waters of the US (WOTUS) – Federally protected water bodies, including navigable rivers, lakes, and wetlands, subject to ACOE jurisdiction.
