Chapter Ten

Inheriting a Project - Navigating the Challenges and Leveraging Opportunities

M&A is the key to filling pipeline gaps. With increasingly complex market and regulatory conditions, treat every renewable acquisition as if you’re buying development execution capability under extreme time pressure, not just development potential under favorable market conditions. This demands a holistic approach, combining meticulous due diligence, strategic collaboration, and proactive risk management.

Acquiring existing projects can offer a shortcut to market entry, but it also presents unique challenges. Understanding how to manage inherited work is essential for realizing the project’s full potential. At some point, all projects will be the subject of a transaction, whether it’s debt for Notice to Proceed (NTP) and start of construction, tax equity at operations, purchase or sale, whatever it might be. Ensuring that everything is ready for financial closing requires thorough due diligence. The following checklist identifies key aspects that must be addressed.

Fast-Transaction Readiness

Developers anticipating potential divestitures or accelerated sales should prepare projects for rapid diligence review by organizing title materials, surveys, permits, lease portfolios, interconnection documentation, and environmental records in advance. Market-ready projects are more likely to transact efficiently under compressed market conditions.

Due Diligence Checklist:

Verify land control and ownership to prevent legal issues. Assess environmental and regional risks early to avoid surprises.

  • Understanding Ownership Complexities: Identify all parties with interests in the property. Ensure all necessary signatories are secured for valid agreements.
  • Navigating Regional Risks: Be aware of state-specific challenges like mineral rights. Engage local experts to provide insights and solutions.
  • Efficient Time Management: Develop strategies to conduct due diligence effectively under tight timelines. Prioritize critical areas without sacrificing thoroughness.
  • Environmental Considerations: Incorporate environmental assessments from the outset. Use public resources and specialist expertise to inform decisions.
  • Enhancing Team Collaboration: Foster communication and cooperation between commercial and development teams. Align objectives and incentives to promote unified efforts.
  • Accurate Valuation: Adjust valuations based on due diligence findings and realistic timelines. Ensure financial assessments reflect true project value and risks.
  • Managing Inherited Projects: Conduct comprehensive audits of inherited work. Plan for resource allocation to address challenges.

Challenges of Inheriting Projects

  • Unknown Quality of Work: Previous developers may have different standards or expertise. Risk: Incomplete or inadequate documentation, permitting issues, or suboptimal design.
  • Stakeholder Relationships: Existing relationships with landowners, regulators, and communities may not transfer smoothly. Risk: Loss of goodwill or misunderstandings.
  • Hidden Issues: Unidentified legal, environmental, or technical problems may surface later. Risk: Unexpected costs and delays.

The biggest handicap that a development team can have is to acquire someone else’s documents and someone else’s work, because you don’t know what you are getting.

Advantages for Inheriting Projects

  • Accelerated Timelines: Bypassing early development stages can bring projects online faster.
  • Cost Savings: Reduced expenditure on initial development activities.
  • Market Access: Immediate presence in desirable locations or markets.

Strategies for Managing Inherited Projects

  • Comprehensive Audit: Review all existing documentation thoroughly. Identify gaps or areas requiring further work.
  • Resource Allocation: Plan for additional resources to address inherited issues. Allocate budget for potential remediation or redesign.
  • Stakeholder Engagement: Reestablish relationships with landowners and authorities. Clearly communicate all ownership changes and future plans.
  • Risk Mitigation: Develop contingency plans for potential problems. Consider purchasing insurance for specific risks.
  • Leverage Existing Assets: Utilize any positive aspects of the previous developer’s work. Build upon established community goodwill where possible.
Tip

Case Study Insight

A company acquired a wind farm project and discovered that community opposition had stalled permitting. By actively engaging with the community, addressing concerns, and making design adjustments, the company turned a challenging situation into a successful project.

Not Every Project Should Be Advanced

In some market environments, developers may determine that certain projects are unlikely to meet revised financing, permitting, or tax-credit timelines. In these situations, early monetization, partial divestiture, or strategic repositioning may preserve value more effectively than continued development.

Conclusion

Inheriting existing projects requires careful management to navigate challenges and capitalize on opportunities. With the right approach, acquired projects can become valuable assets.

The renewable energy sector offers immense opportunities for growth and innovation. By embracing best practices in M&A, companies can navigate complexities, mitigate risks, and contribute to a sustainable energy future.
