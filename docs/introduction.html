Introduction: Why Mergers and Acquisitions are Shaping the Renewable Energy Future

📖 You're reading the condensed, interactive edition. View the full verbatim manuscript mirror →

The renewable energy sector is evolving rapidly, with mergers and acquisitions (M&A) activities playing a pivotal role in shaping the industry’s future. This trend reflects the industry’s maturation and the increasing complexity of developing large-scale renewable projects.

Since entering the renewable energy industry over 30 years ago, I have often seen projects change ownership two or three times before reaching commercial operation. Recently, there has been an upsurge in M&A activity. The termination and restriction of certain Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) tax credits and loan guarantees is impacting renewable energy M&A activity. It has increased deal-making as companies navigate valuation pressure and look for strategic consolidations. It reshapes financing, pushing players toward more risk-hedged, structured deals. And it creates a divide between projects that locked in credits early and those now struggling under new rules.

What we are experiencing now has far-reaching implications. Among them are the following:

Policy Changes & Immediate Effects

The “One Big Beautiful Bill” (OBBB), enacted on July 4, 2025, changes or eliminates certain tax credits that were established by the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA). It also adds Foreign Entity of Concern (FEOC) restrictions to remaining credits. An Executive Order directs Treasury to tighten the definition of “beginning of construction.”

Heightened Uncertainty

The elimination of commonly used safe harbors has thrown eligibility standards into flux, creating ambiguity around what qualifies—causing a rush among developers to accelerate project timelines before credits vanish. Analysts estimate that up to 29 GW of wind and solar projects could fail to meet these tightened deadlines, with as much as 122 GW and $211 billion investment at risk due to the combined effect of phase-outs, tariffs, and regulatory constraints.

Shift toward Opportunistic Acquisitions

With valuations under pressure and financing risk rising for startups and mature projects alike, acquisition activity has surged. Strategic buyers are capitalizing on lower project valuations to make opportunistic purchases. Investors are increasingly favoring “boring but bankable” assets—projects with proven cash flows, minimal risk of policy-related disruption—over speculative, subsidy-reliant technologies.

Rise of Sophisticated Capital Structures

Rather than straightforward equity rounds, stakeholders are turning to layered financing: project finance, debt instruments, preferred equity, or recapitalizations to avoid diluting ownership and remain attractive to potential acquirers.

Strong Underlying Fundamentals Help Sustain M&A

Despite policy reversals, high electricity demand, especially from data centers and electrification trends, continues to support the business case for renewables. Certain tax credits, notably the Investment Tax Credit (ITC) and Production Tax Credit (PTC) applicable to projects initiated before 2025, continue to be available, thereby supporting the preservation of value and reliability for qualifying initiatives.

Investors are acquiring assets at lower valuations due to policy uncertainty. Projects with safe-harbored tax credits retain value; others face steep discounting. Yet rising electricity demand sustains long-term viability, even with policy risks.

The Core Challenge

My work with developers, investors, landowners, and other stakeholders has given me exposure to a wide range of issues that can arise throughout the project lifecycle. Simple oversights can derail the entire project or result in costly delays. About 40% of M&A deals fail, often due to overlooked details discovered only at closing.

As companies strive to meet ambitious pipeline goals and capitalize on emerging opportunities, understanding M&A best practices becomes essential.

Land is a fundamental asset for renewable projects. It is imperative to verify land control to ensure that the project rests on solid ground—literally and legally. Issues may occur if land control details are not meticulously managed, promptly addressed, or adequately considered. Land issues can delay deals, increase legal costs, and disrupt timelines just before closing. Verifying land control is a critical step that safeguards the investment and ensures that the project’s foundation is secure. It is a painstaking process, yet understanding potential pitfalls can arm your team with the tools to identify and mitigate risk.

What This Book Covers

This book will guide you through critical aspects of the M&A process, from verifying land control to fostering collaboration between teams. Although the primary emphasis of this book is on mergers and acquisitions, the expertise and practical insights and strategies presented throughout are relevant across all stages of project development.

Each chapter provides best practices for navigating the complexities of developing large-scale renewable projects. Whether you are an industry veteran or new to the field, this book aims to provide valuable insights into the complexities and nuances of acquiring and developing renewable energy projects.
