Chapter 8: Enhancing Collaboration Between M&A and Development Teams

📖 Condensed edition  ·  Switch to full verbatim manuscript →

Successful project acquisition and development hinge on effective collaboration between commercial (M&A) teams and development teams. The consequences of misalignment include inefficiencies, project delays, cost overruns, and strained workplace relationships. Successful transaction teams establish clear ownership responsibilities, regular status reviews, and structured follow-up procedures to prevent unresolved defects from delaying financing or closing.

Team Alignment Is a Deal Accelerator

Alignment between commercial, development, legal, title, permitting, and financing teams can materially influence transaction outcomes. Misaligned assumptions about timelines, site control, permitting complexity, or construction sequencing often lead to valuation gaps, financing delays, cost overruns, and reduced returns.

Renewable energy closings often depend on centralized transaction management. Unified closing protocols that coordinate legal documentation, title review, surveys, escrow instructions, lender requirements, and execution timelines can significantly reduce closing delays and improve financing certainty. Strategic communications, stakeholder engagement, and political awareness should also be integrated into broader project execution and risk management strategies.

Integrated Expert Teams

Transactions increasingly require early coordination among title professionals, legal counsel, environmental consultants, permitting specialists, engineers, surveyors, and financing teams. Siloed diligence creates conflicting assumptions, delayed issue identification, and avoidable transaction risk. Many developers establish preferred relationships with surveyors, title professionals, environmental consultants, and legal teams familiar with renewable transactions — repeat collaboration improves communication, accelerates issue resolution, and creates greater consistency across projects and portfolios. Integrated milestone calendars align permitting deadlines, title curative milestones, EPC schedules, PPA obligations, financing timelines, and COD targets into a centralized tracking framework shared across commercial and development teams.

Challenges in Team Collaboration

— Different Objectives

Without clear direction, commercial teams won’t necessarily confer with development teams because they operate under different directives. Commercial teams focus on deal closure, acquisition targets, and financial metrics; development teams focus on project feasibility, technical viability, and long-term operational success. This creates misalignment at the outset, which can lead to inefficiencies, increased costs, and a strained culture.

Communication Gaps

Compensation structures may encourage silos instead of teamwork — individuals who work independently may keep knowledge private rather than demonstrate transparency or collaboration. A lack of information sharing leaves development teams unprepared for inherited issues, increasing the risk of delays. Transactions sometimes reach the development team only to be revisited or renegotiated because information wasn’t fully shared along the way.

Warning

Transparency & Collaboration: Lack of transparency or collaboration costs valuable time — particularly under current market conditions, where teams may have only about 24 months to monetize key projects.

Overlooking development concerns leads to unexpected costs and frustration, and when teams feel undervalued or overburdened, morale suffers. Timing is critical: teams need the tools, structure, and environment to work efficiently and effectively.

Strategies for Enhancing Collaboration

  • Early Involvement: Include development team representatives in M&A discussions from the outset; ensure they have input on technical and operational considerations.
  • Regular Communication: Establish routine meetings between teams; use shared platforms for documentation and updates.
  • Aligned Incentives: Create compensation structures that reward both deal closure and successful implementation; encourage team-based performance metrics.
  • Shared Goals: Define common objectives spanning acquisition and development success; foster a culture of collaboration, mutual respect, and a safe space to ask questions.
  • Cross-Functional Training: Provide opportunities for team members to learn about each other’s roles and challenges, and how decisions in one area affect the other.

Collaboration & Communication Improve Outcomes

A collaborative environment increases the likelihood of meeting critical deadlines and staying within budget. Potential risks are identified and mitigated earlier, and a culture of collaboration fosters innovation as diverse perspectives lead to better problem solving. Training programs that encourage broad participation — including team members in ancillary roles — give people the opportunity to learn the many facets of a project. Understanding one another’s roles and challenges leads to greater emotional investment and a more skilled, dependable team — connecting with one another as human beings is foundational to optimal operational performance.

Benefits of Effective Collaboration

  • Improved Project Outcomes: Projects are more likely to meet timelines and budget when teams work cohesively.
  • Risk Mitigation: Potential issues are identified and addressed early.
  • Enhanced Innovation: Diverse perspectives lead to better problem-solving.

Key Takeaways

  • Commercial and development teams operate under different incentives by default — without intentional alignment, that difference becomes a transaction risk.
  • A unified closing protocol that synchronizes legal, title, survey, escrow, and lender requirements reduces delays more effectively than any single team working in isolation.
  • Cross-functional training and shared milestone calendars convert structural misalignment into structural collaboration.
  • Compressed monetization windows (as short as 24 months in some markets) make communication gaps especially costly.

Discussion Questions

  1. What specific incentive misalignments tend to separate commercial and development teams, and how would you redesign compensation to close that gap?
  2. Why does a unified closing protocol reduce risk more than simply asking teams to “communicate more”?
  3. How would you onboard a development team to a project they are inheriting mid-transaction to minimize the loss of institutional knowledge?
