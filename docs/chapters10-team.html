Chapter 10: Team Collaboration – Building the Deal Team That Closes

Why Deal Teams Fail

A renewable energy M&A transaction involves lawyers, engineers, environmental consultants, title professionals, financial analysts, tax advisors, and operations specialists—all working simultaneously on interrelated workstreams. When these professionals work in silos, the deal suffers.

A developer’s legal team had negotiated an asset purchase structure that the tax team had not reviewed. When the tax team finally reviewed the draft PSA, they identified that the asset purchase structure triggered a $4.2 million state transfer tax liability that had not been modeled. The buyer’s offer was priced for an equity transaction. Renegotiating the structure delayed closing by 45 days and nearly killed the deal.

The most effective deal teams are integrated from day one—not assembled piecemeal as issues arise.

The Core Deal Team

A complete renewable energy M&A deal team includes the following roles:

Transaction Counsel (Lead Legal)

The lead attorney manages the overall transaction, drafts and negotiates the PSA, coordinates third-party consents, and manages the closing. In complex deals, separate counsel may handle regulatory, environmental, and tax matters.

Key responsibilities: - PSA drafting and negotiation - Third-party consent coordination - Closing checklist management - Representations and warranties insurance engagement

Financial Advisor / Investment Banker

For larger transactions, a financial advisor manages the sale process, prepares the offering memorandum, runs the bid process, and assists in valuation negotiation.

Key responsibilities: - Process management and bidder communication - Valuation analysis and defense - Financing sourcing (equity and debt) - Closing coordination

Independent Engineer (IE)

The IE reviews construction plans, equipment specifications, and operating history to provide an independent assessment of the project’s technical condition and energy production estimates.

Key responsibilities: - Technical due diligence report - Energy yield review (P50/P90 validation) - Construction risk assessment - Equipment condition assessment

Environmental Consultant

The environmental consultant conducts Phase I and Phase II ESAs, biological surveys, and wetland delineations.

Key responsibilities: - Phase I ESA - Phase II ESA (if RECs identified) - Biological surveys (avian, bat, species) - Wetland delineation and jurisdictional determination

Title Company and Land Professionals

The title team reviews the chain of title, issues title commitments, and confirms land control.

Key responsibilities: - Title search and commitment - Survey review - Mineral rights investigation - Title insurance coordination

Tax Advisor

Tax counsel reviews the transaction structure, models tax implications, confirms incentive eligibility, and coordinates with tax equity investors.

Key responsibilities: - Transaction structure tax analysis - ITC/PTC eligibility confirmation - Tax equity agreement review - State and local tax analysis

Operations Lead

The operations specialist manages SCADA transition, O&M contract review, and post-close integration planning.

Key responsibilities: - O&M contract due diligence - SCADA and performance review - Day 1 readiness planning - Post-close integration checklist

Deal Team Collaboration Framework

The Communication Architecture

Effective deal teams share information deliberately—not haphazardly:

Weekly Deal Team Calls

A standing weekly call with all workstream leads ensures: - Issues surface early (before they become crises) - Interdependencies between workstreams are identified - Timeline risks are flagged and managed - Decision-making is centralized

The Issues Log

A shared issues log tracks every open diligence item, assigned owner, target resolution date, and status. The issues log: - Prevents items from falling through the cracks - Creates accountability across the team - Provides a record for post-closing indemnity claims - Documents the basis for purchase price adjustments

The Data Room

The data room is the organized repository of all project documents. Effective data room management requires: - Consistent folder structure (legal, financial, technical, environmental, land, permits) - Version control for documents that change during diligence - Access log (who viewed what, when) - Index that maps documents to due diligence checklist items

Managing Interdisciplinary Tensions

Deal teams include professionals from different disciplines with different training, priorities, and risk tolerances. Common tensions include:

Legal vs. Financial: Lawyers see risk; financial analysts see return. Lawyers may recommend conditions and indemnities that deal economists view as deal-killers. Finding the right risk/return balance requires explicit discussion—not professional turf protection.

Engineering vs. Schedule: Independent engineers provide thorough, conservative assessments. Buyers seeking to close quickly may push to shorten IE timelines. Compressing IE review increases the risk of missing material technical issues.

Environmental vs. Deal Momentum: Environmental consultants are trained to be cautious. A Phase II recommendation that delays closing by 60 days can create friction. The solution is to start environmental work early—not to rush it at the end.

Tax vs. Structure: Tax implications of deal structure are frequently underweighted until the final stages of negotiation. Engaging tax counsel early—at LOI stage—prevents late-stage structural renegotiation.

Cross-Disciplinary Learning Objectives

Students in law, business, engineering, and environmental policy bring different lenses to renewable energy M&A. This textbook is designed to develop cross-disciplinary literacy:

Your Discipline Core Skills You Bring What You Need to Learn
Law Contract drafting, risk allocation, regulatory compliance Financial modeling, technical performance metrics, O&M economics
Business/Finance Valuation, capital structure, deal economics Permitting timelines, technical risk, environmental liability quantification
Engineering Technical performance, equipment assessment, construction risk Contract interpretation, tax equity structures, deal timeline management
Environmental Science Environmental risk identification, regulatory compliance Financial impact of environmental findings, permitting economics
Policy Regulatory landscape, incentive programs Transaction structure, deal mechanics, financial modeling

Best Practices for Team Collaboration

  1. Assemble the Team at LOI: Do not wait until the PSA phase to engage counsel, IE, and environmental consultants. Parallel workstreams save time.

  2. Establish a Single Point of Contact: All team communications should flow through the transaction lead. Parallel conversations between buyer and seller subject matter experts create confusion and risk.

  3. Use a Shared Issues Log: Assign every open item an owner and a deadline. Review the log at every weekly team call.

  4. Integrate Early: Tax counsel should review deal structure at LOI stage. The IE should be engaged at Phase I, not Phase III. Environmental consultants should be on-site before the PSA is signed.

  5. Celebrate Discipline Differences: The best renewable energy deal professionals are those who can translate across disciplines—understanding both the technical risk and the financial implication, both the legal exposure and the operational reality.

Key Takeaways

  • Renewable energy M&A requires a multidisciplinary team working in parallel—not in sequence.
  • The transaction lead coordinates all workstreams and manages information flow.
  • Weekly deal team calls and a shared issues log are the most effective tools for managing complex, multi-party due diligence.
  • Disciplinary tensions (legal vs. financial, engineering vs. schedule) are predictable and can be managed with explicit communication norms.
  • Cross-disciplinary literacy—understanding how each discipline’s work affects the others—is the hallmark of an effective deal professional.

Discussion Questions

  2. What is an “issues log” and how does it protect buyers both during diligence and post-closing?
  3. Describe a scenario where a legal recommendation and a financial recommendation conflict, and how a transaction lead should resolve the conflict.
  4. How does the interdisciplinary nature of renewable energy M&A differ from a traditional corporate acquisition of a manufacturing company?
  5. What skills would you, in your own discipline, need to develop to be an effective contributor to a renewable energy M&A deal team?
